### 1. BUG-55 IS A DEPLOYMENT DEFECT, NOT A PRODUCT BUG (read this one)
The single most valuable finding of the session, and it came from a PO remark, not from
process. The brief for B4 was about to have an agent BUILD city country/state autopopulation.
Ryan said "country and state/region as that's how it was working previously" — one sentence
that reclassified the whole item. Re-probing on that basis found:

- The feature is FULLY BUILT. `AddPlaceFlow.tsx:183-208` auto-populates country AND region.
- `useCities.ts:46` — the BROWSER fetches `nominatim.openstreetmap.org` directly.
- `server.ts:128-130` — helmet's CSP `connectSrc` is `[self, *.maptiler.com, CLERK_ORIGIN]`.
  Nominatim is absent. So the call is CSP-blocked in any served environment, and
  `AddPlaceFlow.tsx:206`'s bare `.catch()` swallows it with no feedback and no logging.
- It works under the Vite dev server, which applies no such CSP. Hence "working previously,"
  and hence no test caught it: every test runs the dev/jsdom path.

Second defect found in the same function: `useCities.ts:47` sets a `User-Agent` header on a
browser `fetch()` — a FORBIDDEN HEADER NAME, silently dropped, leaving the app anonymous
against Nominatim's usage policy. Both point at a backend proxy rather than a widened
allowlist, which is a SEC-01 call. BUG-55 was PULLED from B4 and re-owned to Architect.

**The COO's own failure here, recorded deliberately:** BUG-55's tracker note had said "likely
the geocode/lookup path isn't wired" since 2026-07-21 — an unverified single-probe absence
claim already covered by the negative-findings rule, disproved by one grep. Ryan generously
offered that the fault was his for labelling UAT feedback without distinguishing bug from
feature request. It wasn't the primary cause. The rule existed and was not applied.

### 3. THE ANSWER TO "BAD TESTS OR BAD ENVIRONMENT?" — it's the environment, specifically
Ryan asked whether we should mirror staging/prod more closely, since the suite passed when it
should have failed. It is the environment, and the difference is nameable in one line:

**Production is single-origin; E2E is two-origin.**
- Prod: `server.ts:233` mounts `express.static(DIST_DIR)` under `NODE_ENV=production`, so
  Express serves the frontend DOCUMENT and helmet's CSP applies to it.
- E2E: `playwright.config.ts` serves the frontend from `vite preview --port 5173` with Express
  separately on `:3001`. The document under test carries NO CSP header at all.

CSP binds the document's origin, so nothing is ever blocked in E2E. That entire defect class is
invisible by construction — no amount of test-writing inside that environment finds it. CI also
sets `GEOCODING_ENABLED=false`, a second layer of the same blind spot.

Tracked as QUAL-18 (P1, single-origin E2E topology), QUAL-19 (static CSP-allowlist contract
test — CHEAPEST, ~1hr, would have caught BUG-55 on its own, do this first), QUAL-20 (post-deploy
smoke check — belongs in CI, where D-06's firewall limitation does NOT apply).
