================================================================================
WHAT THIS THREAD WAS
================================================================================
A re-design after outright rejection. The QUAL-25 spike proposed widening the
cities unique index with quantised coordinates (ROUND(lat,1), ROUND(lng,1)); the
OP-27 fresh-eyes review killed it with three independently fatal findings, the
decisive one being that the index NEVER FIRES — findOrUpgradeCity pass 1 decides
identity in a SELECT, before resolveCityName, with no coordinates to bind.

I was given five acceptance criteria and told to answer each explicitly, plus one
genuinely open question the earlier work never asked: WHICH gazetteer.

================================================================================
WHERE I LANDED
================================================================================
Identity is CARRIED, not DERIVED. A nullable cities.place_ref TEXT, namespaced,
supplied in the create request because the user picked a specific row from a list.
Known before the insert, written exactly once, never touched by the geocoder.
Coordinates leave the identity question entirely and go back to being data.

Two indexes:
  UNIQUE(name COLLATE NOCASE, country_code, COALESCE(region_id,0), COALESCE(place_ref,''))
  UNIQUE(place_ref) WHERE place_ref IS NOT NULL
plus CHECK (place_ref IS NULL OR length(place_ref) > 0) — load-bearing, see below.

New step 0 in findOrUpgradeCity, ahead of every existing step and ahead of the
geocoder. Steps 1/2/2b unchanged and reached only when no place_ref is supplied.

DATASET: keep cities.json. Do not swap. Do not join a donor id. Mint place_ref as
'cj1:' + first 16 hex of sha256(country|admin1|admin2|name|lat|lng).

================================================================================
THE THING I WOULD TELL THE NEXT ARCHITECT FIRST
================================================================================
"cities.json has no stable row id" is TRUE. "cities.json has no identity" is FALSE.
Everyone (ADL-48, the spike, the review, the brief) made the second claim from the
first, and the coordinate bucket exists entirely because of it.

It has no id COLUMN. It has a NATURAL KEY, and I measured it unique over every one
of its 170,540 rows — zero duplicates. The app has simply been discarding admin2,
which is precisely the field that separates the four Newports:

  GB|ENG|E1|Newport  (East Riding)   GB|ENG|O2|Newport  (Shropshire)
  GB|ENG|E4|Newport  (Essex)         GB|ENG|G2|Newport  (Isle of Wight)

A verified negative finding can still license an UNVERIFIED inference. The probe
was right; the sentence after the probe was not. Check the step AFTER the probe.

FOUR OF MY OWN FINDINGS DISPROVED BEFORE FILING
================================================================================
1. "all-the-cities cityId churns badly — 740 ids repoint to a different place."
   FALSE and backwards. They are RENAMES (AM/Ejmiatsin -> AM/Vagharshapat) with the
   id correctly following the place through the rename. Real figure 0.089%. I would
   have overstated the case against an option I rejected anyway, for the wrong reason.
   The bug in my probe: matching forward by id and calling any name change a repoint.
2. "The admin-only key is the elegant answer." It churns 3x less so I wanted it.
   584 duplicate groups. Not unique. Discarded.
3. "Make the natural-key index partial (WHERE place_ref IS NULL)." SQLite cannot use
   a partial index for a query whose WHERE omits the partial condition, so the tail's
   step-1 lookup would lose its index. The uglier COALESCE form is correct.
4. "A place_ref miss should fall back to the natural key — more forgiving." This was
   in my first draft of step 0. It IS BUG-75: a user who named a specific place gets
   handed a different one. Removed and promoted to the design's top invariant.

================================================================================
WHAT I FLAGGED AS MY OWN WEAK POINTS (§8, for the OP-27 reviewer)
================================================================================
A1 (start here). The index does NOT prevent a user-typed row coexisting with a
    gazetteer row for the same real place (T5). Pass-1 policy must. That is the SAME
    CLASS of weakness F2 destroyed the previous design over — identity decided in
    application code. My defence is narrower than "fixed": the index now AGREES with
    the policy instead of contradicting it, and the discriminator is immutable and
    present at the decision point. A policy bug still yields a duplicate — it just no
    longer yields a wrong town. Attack: find a request sequence producing two rows.
A2. The dataset decision rests on TWO n=1 version-pair comparisons over
    non-comparable windows (2 months in 2026 vs 4 months in 2019-20). Read "5x more
    stable" as an order of magnitude, not a constant.
A3. Step 0d's adoption inherits D13 step 2's repoint hazard (upgrading a pending row
    already attached to a trip place silently moves the trip). Same scoping, already
    accepted in ADL-46, not widened — but least load-bearing decision in the document
    and can be flipped without touching anything else.

   (GeoNames CC BY 4.0, plus OSM and Natural Earth).
4. SEQUENCING COST. S0 claimed to fix BUG-75 independently of the gazetteer. This does
   not — E1/E2 ship inert and the defect stops at E3. Possible decoupling flagged, not
   designed: capture Nominatim's osm_type+osm_id as 'osm:<type>:<id>' in the D14
   candidate flow. UNVERIFIED against a live response (host firewalled). Use
   osm_type+osm_id, NEVER place_id (documented unstable across reimports).
5. DOWNLOAD.GEONAMES.ORG is unreachable from this devcontainer (curl + the allowlist in
   .devcontainer/init-firewall.sh — two probes that fail differently). That is the
   container's reachability, not the host's or CI's. If a GeoNames dump can be vendored
   from outside the container, 'geonames:<geonameid>' is strictly better than 'cj1:<hash>'
   and the namespace prefix exists precisely so that switch is additive, not a migration.
